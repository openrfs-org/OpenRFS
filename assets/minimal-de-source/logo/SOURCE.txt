THE MARK, AND THE TWO FILES THE BUILD ACTUALLY READS.

openrfs-logo-2026.jpeg is the OpenRFS mark: Saud Aljuaid's own drawing
of the fish, 1280 x 1197, sha256

  5c56d4a31633479aa61d8e440e2ee97d3e4b15365160dd5a8378e666d6265e8b

It is the same file as 19864919-cute-red-fish-cartoon.jpeg in the
openrfs command line repository, copied untouched.

openrfs-mark-2026-flat.png is that drawing with its ground flattened and
a margin put round it: every pixel at luminance 250 or above set to pure
white, cropped to the ink, twenty pixels of white either side, doubled.
The flattening is not cosmetic. The JPEG's background is not one value -
it runs 251 to 255, which is compression ripple around a white ground -
and both of the tools below treat "pure white" as "nothing here", so two
levels of ripple is the difference between a clean silhouette and a
picture with a texture in it.

  (A note against a wrong turn taken here: stretching that 251-255 band
  across full black and white makes the ripple look like a tiled
  watermark, and it was briefly read as one. It is not. A twenty-level
  window amplified twelve times will do that to any JPEG.)

openrfs-logo-2026.json is the ART, and it is NOT this project's own
work:

  Tool:     https://coddy.tech/tools/ascii-art-generator
  Mode:     Image
  Input:    openrfs-logo-2026.jpeg
  Settings: Color ON, Invert OFF, WIDTH exactly 45, everything else at
            its default
  Taken:    18 September 2026

It was run in a browser driven by Playwright and captured whole - every
character and the computed colour of every character, out of the page's
own <pre class="AsciiArt_asciiBlock"> - so it can be redone: set those
three controls, upload that file, read the block back.

WHY A CAPTURE AND NOT A PIPELINE. The mark used to be built in two
halves. The characters came from asciiart.eu's image-to-ascii with its
"Black and White" set, and the colours were computed here by classifying
the drawing into five things - paper, outline, body, tongue, eye - and
giving each cell whichever class held most of it.

Reading that converter's own source settles what the first half was
doing: its "Black and White" set is

    blockelement: "█"

one character, plus a space for the gradient's other end, chosen by
`floor(luminance * 1 / 255)`. It is a SILHOUETTE, thresholded at pure
white. Every feature the mark has - the eyes, the shading, the line
between the fins - had to be put back afterwards by the classifier,
which is why there was a classifier, why it had five hand-tuned
thresholds, and why two of those thresholds were measured wrong at
different times and nobody could see it.

coddy.tech's generator does both halves at once: a character per cell
out of a real gradient (` .:-=+*%#`) and a colour per cell out of the
source pixels. So there is nothing here to classify and nothing to
average. tools/make-logo.py reduces the capture's 251 distinct colours
to a palette of fifteen by k-means - a terminal cell carries one byte of
attribute, not three - and emits the characters, the indices and the
palette. Every cell on screen is the cell that generator produced.

The harness checks this the only way that means anything: it counts how
many of the fifteen reach the framebuffer, and fails unless all of them
do. A mark that arrives with fifteen colours and leaves with four has
been flattened somewhere between the table and the glass.

WHAT IS NOT HERE ANY MORE.

openrfs-logo-source.jpeg and openrfs-mark-flat.png were the 2025 drawing
and its flat build, and openrfs-mark-512.png was the website's build of
it. Nothing read any of them once the mark above replaced them.

onion.png was the mark before the fish, and trait.svg was the mark
before that - the menu button on a panel this desktop no longer has.
files.svg was its own file-manager icon, replaced when the gentoo set
was vendored. wallpaper.png was the desktop's background, and the root
has been the X server's own weave since the fvwm pass: nothing has
decoded an image for it in a long time, and tools/make-wallpaper.py went
with it.

src/trait_mark.h went too. It was generated by tools/make-app-icons.py,
which is not in the tree, no .c file included it, and its header comment
still named a project this one has not been called for months.

The Trait theme in src/theme.c is still in the list, and its comment
still says its two colours were sampled from the onion and from the
wallpaper. That remains true of where the numbers came from; it is no
longer something a reader of this repository can check for themselves,
and the comment says so.
